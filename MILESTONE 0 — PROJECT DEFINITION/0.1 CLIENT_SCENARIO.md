# Client Scenario Definition

## 1. Target Business

### Business Type
Residential HVAC and home-comfort services company.

### Example Company Profile
A small-to-medium local service business that installs, repairs, and maintains heating, cooling, and indoor comfort systems for homeowners.

### Business Model
- Service-based revenue model
- Paid jobs from repair, maintenance, and replacement work
- High-value appointments are critical to revenue
- Most sales come from urgent inbound requests

## 2. Ideal Client Profile

The ideal client is a local business that:

- serves a specific metro area or city
- receives leads through its website, Google Ads, or local SEO
- depends on phone calls and form submissions to book service visits
- has a small team of sales / dispatch / technician staff
- loses money when qualified leads are missed or delayed

## 3. Lead Sources

The business receives leads from multiple channels:

- website contact form
- Google Business Profile calls and messages
- Facebook / Instagram lead ads
- local SEO and Google search traffic
- manual call-ins from referrals

## 4. Lead Journey

A homeowner sees a need such as a broken AC unit, weak airflow, or no heat.

Then the journey usually looks like this:

1. Visitor submits a form or calls the company
2. Team receives the enquiry manually
3. Staff asks for service details and urgency
4. Lead is vetted for location, problem type, budget, and timing
5. A technician or dispatcher tries to book an appointment
6. The customer is contacted again for confirmation or rescheduling
7. Service is performed and the experience is followed up

## 5. Current Manual Process

The current process is often reactive and inconsistent:

- lead comes in via form or phone
- receptionist or office admin responds manually
- follow-up depends on whoever is available
- some leads are missed if the phone is busy or calls are not answered quickly
- duplicate leads can be created from repeated contact attempts
- inconsistent qualification means weak leads get treated the same as strong ones
- scheduling is done on a shared calendar or by message threads
- value is lost when a lead has to wait too long for a response

## 6. Business Pain

### Operational Pain
- Slow response time to inbound lead requests
- Leads are missed during busy periods
- Staff manually re-enter contact details across multiple systems
- Inconsistent qualification leads to poor scheduling decisions
- Lack of prioritization for urgent service calls

### Financial Pain
- Lost revenue from unhandled or delayed leads
- Missed opportunities during peak service demand
- Wasted staff time on low-quality leads
- Poor follow-up reduces conversion rates

### Customer Experience Pain
- Customers expect fast responses, especially for HVAC emergencies
- Long response delays create frustration and drive customers to competitors
- A slow process makes the business look unreliable

## 7. Desired Outcome

The business wants a system that:

- captures every lead consistently
- validates required fields quickly
- identifies the lead's urgency and service need
- checks if the lead is already in the system
- routes the customer to the correct dispatcher or sales rep
- schedules an appointment automatically where possible
- confirms the appointment and sends reminders
- keeps follow-up moving even outside business hours

## 8. Appointment Requirement

The appointment is the key business event.

The automation should support this requirement:

- lead must be responded to within minutes, not hours
- urgent calls should be prioritized above general maintenance requests
- appointments should be booked within a realistic timeframe such as 24–72 hours
- the customer should receive confirmation and reminder messages
- no lead should be lost due to missed phone calls or delayed manual processing

## 9. Automation Opportunity

This is a strong fit for an n8n-driven lead-to-appointment workflow because the process is predictable and high-value.

### Proposed automation flow

Webhook / lead form
→ Validate required data
→ Normalize fields
→ Check for duplicate or previous inquiry
→ Store lead in a database or spreadsheet
→ Score or classify the lead
→ Route to correct team or sales queue
→ Notify staff or send internal alert
→ Draft or send AI-assisted follow-up message
→ Book appointment in calendar or scheduling tool
→ Send confirmation and reminder messages

## 10. Why This Is a Strong Portfolio Project

This scenario is realistic, understandable, and sellable because it demonstrates:

- business process understanding
- lead capture and qualification
- AI-assisted response logic
- calendar or scheduling integration
- CRM-style lead management
- follow-up and reminder automation
- clear ROI from faster response and higher appointment conversion

## 11. Recommended Project Positioning

This project should be positioned as:

"AI-powered lead capture and appointment automation for a local home-services business."

This keeps the work focused on a real service business problem rather than a vague automation demo.

## 12. Decision Summary

The business chosen for this project is a local HVAC / home-services company because it offers:

- urgent demand
- predictable lead flow
- obvious business pain
- clear automation ROI
- a plausible service offering for a freelancer or automation consultant

This is the most practical scenario to build and explain as the foundation of the project.
