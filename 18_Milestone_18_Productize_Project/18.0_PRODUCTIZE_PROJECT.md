# Milestone 18 — Productize the Project

## Objective

Turn the Voryn project into a clearly defined automation service that can be explained, scoped, priced, delivered, maintained, and adapted for similar service businesses.

Productization must be based on accepted implementation and portfolio evidence. Do not sell features that remain untested, unsecured, or undocumented.

## 18.0 Service Definition

### Working service name

```text
Lead-to-Appointment Automation Setup
```

### Service promise

Help a small service business capture website inquiries, organize lead data, qualify opportunities, prepare timely responses, and move suitable prospects toward appointments with human oversight.

The promise is operational, not autonomous: the system reduces repetitive work and makes the lead journey visible while keeping important customer communication under human control.

### Core outcome

```text
More consistent lead handling
+ faster internal response
+ less manual data entry
+ clearer follow-up ownership
+ better appointment visibility
```

### Current supported capability

The project demonstrates:

- website or webhook lead capture;
- deterministic validation and normalization;
- Supabase lead storage;
- Gemini-assisted analysis and response drafting;
- deterministic scoring and routing;
- internal notifications;
- human approval before important customer communication;
- follow-up state tracking;
- Calendly appointment processing;
- error handling, security review, testing, deployment, and monitoring plans.

Production claims require the release gates from Milestones 12–15 to be accepted.

## 18.1 Ideal Client

### Primary ideal client

A small service business that:

- receives regular website inquiries;
- handles leads manually through email, spreadsheets, or a lightweight CRM;
- has a meaningful appointment or consultation outcome;
- loses time to copying, qualifying, routing, and following up;
- has an owner or staff member who can approve customer communication;
- does not yet need a complex enterprise CRM implementation.

### Suitable examples

- web design and marketing agencies;
- consultants and coaches;
- home-service businesses;
- local professional services;
- small B2B service providers;
- appointment-based specialists.

### Poor fit

The service is not a strong fit when the client:

- expects fully autonomous negotiation or customer messaging;
- requires regulated-data workflows without an appropriate compliance review;
- needs high-volume enterprise infrastructure;
- requires an unsupported CRM or communication channel as a hard dependency;
- cannot provide approved service information, response rules, or an operational owner.

## 18.3 Service Packages

Use packages to make scope understandable. Exact prices require research and should not be invented from this portfolio project.

### Package A — Lead Intake Foundation

Includes:

- form or webhook capture;
- validation and normalization;
- storage;
- basic internal notification;
- handover documentation.

Best for a client replacing manual copy-and-paste work first.

### Package B — Qualified Lead Workflow

Includes Package A plus:

- AI-assisted message analysis;
- deterministic scoring and classification;
- route-specific notifications;
- response drafting;
- human approval workflow;
- focused test report.

Best for a client that needs consistent qualification and response preparation.

### Package C — Lead-to-Appointment System

Includes Package B plus:

- follow-up state tracking;
- Calendly appointment processing;
- confirmation and reminder paths;
- error handling and escalation;
- security review;
- deployment and monitoring documentation.

Best for a client with an established appointment process and an operational owner.

### Add-ons

Potential add-ons should be quoted separately:

- CRM integration;
- additional lead sources;
- alternate email or messaging provider;
- calendar provider integration;
- custom scoring rules;
- dashboard or reporting views;
- migration of existing lead data;
- additional approval roles;
- ongoing monitoring and maintenance;
- staff training and workflow optimization.

## 18.4 Scope Boundaries

### Included by default

- one business process;
- one primary lead source;
- one storage system;
- one AI provider;
- one scheduling provider;
- one internal notification path;
- one approval process;
- test data and documented acceptance cases;
- agreed configuration and handover.

### Excluded unless quoted

- autonomous customer negotiation;
- legal, medical, financial, or other regulated decision-making;
- guaranteed lead conversion or revenue results;
- custom CRM replacement;
- 24/7 incident response;
- unlimited revisions;
- production hosting fees and provider subscriptions;
- copywriting beyond agreed response templates;
- importing unverified historical data;
- security or compliance certifications;
- unsupported provider integrations.

Document any exception before implementation begins.

## 18.5 Discovery Questions

Use discovery to confirm fit and scope:

1. Where do leads currently arrive?
2. What information is required before a lead can be contacted?
3. What makes a lead high priority?
4. What response must always require human approval?
5. What appointment tool is currently used?
6. What happens when a lead does not respond?
7. What counts as a duplicate?
8. Who owns manual review and incidents?
9. What data may be sent to an AI provider?
10. What systems and credentials are available?
11. What volume and response-time expectations exist?
12. What would make the project a success after 30, 60, and 90 days?

## 18.7 Delivery Process

```text
1. Discovery and fit check
        ↓
2. Process map and success criteria
        ↓
3. Scope and proposal
        ↓
4. Test data and approved business rules
        ↓
5. Build in development
        ↓
6. Client review in staging
        ↓
7. Security and end-to-end testing
        ↓
8. Production deployment
        ↓
9. Handover and training
        ↓
10. Monitoring and maintenance
```

Do not move directly from an exported workflow to production without the testing, security, backup, and rollback gates.

## 18.8 Maintenance Offer

Offer maintenance only when responsibilities are explicit:

- execution and error review;
- provider credential rotation;
- workflow updates;
- prompt and business-rule adjustments;
- backup and restore checks;
- alert threshold review;
- minor configuration changes;
- incident triage and escalation.

Define:

```text
Support hours:
Response target:
Included changes:
Excluded changes:
Emergency handling:
Provider fees:
Data access:
Cancellation terms:
Handover on termination:
```

Do not promise a response target that the operating setup cannot support.

## 18.9 Customization Rules

A customization request should be evaluated for:

- business value;
- data and privacy impact;
- provider reliability;
- workflow complexity;
- test coverage;
- maintenance burden;
- rollback difficulty;
- cost and timeline.

Every approved customization should update the workflow documentation, test matrix, security review, deployment record, and maintenance runbook where applicable.

- human-in-the-loop design;

Do not claim:

- guaranteed conversion increases;
- fully autonomous sales;
