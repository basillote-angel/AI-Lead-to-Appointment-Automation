# Milestone 17 — Portfolio Demonstration

## Objective

Present the Voryn automation as a clear, believable portfolio project that demonstrates business thinking, workflow design, AI boundaries, reliability, security awareness, and measurable progress from lead inquiry to appointment.

The demonstration must use accepted test evidence. Do not present planned production behavior, unresolved defects, or fictional test results as completed implementation.

## Demonstration Audience

The portfolio artifact should be understandable to:

- a potential client evaluating automation value;
- an employer evaluating workflow, data, and troubleshooting skills;
- a technical reviewer evaluating architecture and implementation quality.

Lead with the business outcome, then show the technical evidence that supports it.

## 17.0 Demo Scenario

Use one sanitized fictional lead throughout the main demonstration:

```text
Lead: Maria Santos
Business: Santos Creative Co.
Need: Website redesign and improved mobile conversion
Service: Website Redesign
Budget: customer-provided test value
Timeline: customer-provided test value
Message: realistic but fictional inquiry
Expected classification: WARM or HOT, based on the accepted test record
Expected outcome: approved response, scheduling invitation, Calendly booking, confirmation, and follow-up stop
```

Record the exact test data and expected classification in the test report. Do not change the scenario between screenshots without explaining why.

### Demo flow

```text
1. Customer submits inquiry
        ↓
2. Webhook receives and validates lead
        ↓
3. Valid lead is stored in Supabase
        ↓
4. Gemini extracts structured analysis
        ↓
5. Deterministic score and classification are saved
        ↓
6. Internal notification and route are created
        ↓
7. AI response draft is generated
        ↓
8. Human reviews and approves the response
        ↓
9. Lead enters follow-up and scheduling state
        ↓
10. Calendly booking is detected and matched
        ↓
11. Appointment and confirmation are recorded
        ↓
12. Follow-up stops and monitoring evidence is available
```

Show where the workflow stops or escalates when an input, AI result, provider event, or notification is unsafe.

## 17.4 Case Study

Use this case-study structure:

### Title

A clear outcome-oriented title, such as:

```text
AI-Assisted Lead-to-Appointment Automation for a Service Business
```

### Context

Describe Voryn as a fictional portfolio client and explain the manual lead-handling problem.

### Challenge

Explain delays, repetitive data entry, inconsistent qualification, follow-up risk, and scheduling friction.

### Solution

Describe the combined system:

- n8n orchestration
- Supabase storage
- Gemini interpretation and drafting
- deterministic scoring and routing
- human approval
- Calendly scheduling
- notifications, error handling, security, and monitoring

### Workflow

Use one simple diagram and the Maria Santos demonstration journey.

### Technical decisions

Explain why:

- deterministic validation and scoring are kept outside the AI;
- human approval is required for important customer communication;
- Calendly owns availability;
- provider event IDs and lead IDs support idempotency;
- error handling uses bounded retries and escalation;
- monitoring tracks both system and business outcomes.

### Results

Use only accepted test or production metrics. If production metrics do not exist, report verified test observations instead of inventing conversion numbers.

### Limitations

State unresolved or deferred items, such as:

- incomplete production deployment;
- pending security evidence;
- polling instead of a Calendly webhook;
- pending cancellation/rescheduling implementation;
- test-only reminder delays;
- remaining M10/M11 defects, if still open.

### Reflection

Explain what was learned about business requirements, data integrity, AI boundaries, APIs, reliability, and operational ownership.
