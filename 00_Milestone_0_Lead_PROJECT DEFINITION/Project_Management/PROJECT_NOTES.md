# Project Notes

## Why this client scenario fits the project

We chose a local HVAC / home-services business instead of a generic SaaS lead flow because it creates a clear, realistic sales problem with a visible financial impact.

This kind of business receives high-intent inbound leads from people who need immediate help. The value is not abstract; it is measured in booked appointments, reduced missed leads, and faster response times. That makes it suitable for a portfolio automation project.

## Why we are not building workflows yet

The project must stay business-first. Before choosing nodes, triggers, or integrations, we need to understand:

- who the client is
- what problem they are solving
- how leads currently move through the business
- what outcome they actually want from automation

Without this context, the automation would be technically impressive but strategically weak.

## Scenario decision

The target client is a residential HVAC and home-comfort service business.

This scenario is strong because it has:

- a clear lead source
- a high-value appointment outcome
- a visible manual bottleneck
- a clear role for AI and workflow automation
- easy-to-explain business value for a client or employer

## What we learned from this discovery step

A lead-to-appointment automation is valuable when the business has:

- many inbound inquiries
- long response times
- inconsistent qualification
- manual scheduling friction
- dependence on phone-driven follow-up

The automation should not replace the human decision-maker. It should speed up the process, standardize the qualification flow, and make sure promising leads are followed up quickly.

## Key project principle for the next steps

Every future implementation should answer the question:

"What business result does this workflow improve?"

If a tool or workflow adds complexity without a clear business gain, it should be reconsidered.
